## a. Anthropic AI Safety Fellows: an empirical scalable-oversight and AI-control study

Most AI oversight assumes a reviewer at a desk with time to read. Real oversight increasingly happens on a phone, between other tasks, by someone whose attention is already spent. Reflexive is a governed research agent I built and use every day for a longitudinal autoethnography: it captures, structures and provisionally codes evidence, and a human must approve every interpretation and every release. I am turning its iPhone layer into a controlled oversight experiment. Review items are randomly routed to phone or desktop. A sealed, commit-reveal process plants unsupported claims and wrong codes at a hidden rate. The study then measures seeded-error catch rate, review time, rubber-stamping and unsupported-claim escape by device. Alongside, a red-team suite tries to break the agent's four refusal rules through mobile-specific channels: voice, automation, offline replay, and instructions hidden inside captured text. Control is layered, not trusted: deterministic gates hold missing sources and block any release from the phone, whatever the model says. The pilot is one participant, and I report it as one. What travels is the instrument, the mechanisms (which errors escape, at what dwell times), and a demanding existence test: oversight by its own designer, who knows errors are planted. The next step adds participants and a model-overseer arm facing the same planted errors.

## b. Generator Residency: research-integrity infrastructure for the AI safety ecosystem

AI safety research increasingly rests on evidence that someone lived through: red-team sessions, user studies, incident notes, a researcher's own logs. That evidence is usually the least governed part of the pipeline. Reflexive is infrastructure for making such evidence trustworthy. Raw material is immutable and hashed. Every item carries a fidelity label saying whether it is a direct capture or a reconstruction from memory. Interpretation stays separate from evidence. Refusal rules hold claims that lack a source and block publication of identities. Every human approval is logged. I built it as a neurodivergent researcher who does most of my work on a phone during a bus commute, so it is designed for the conditions where integrity usually fails: low attention, interruptions, lost context. It also measures whether its own human checkpoints still work under those conditions, using planted errors that never touch the record or a release. In the residency I would harden it into an open, reusable kit: capture, provenance, refusal tests and an oversight audit, so that safety researchers can show not only what they found but how they know it.
